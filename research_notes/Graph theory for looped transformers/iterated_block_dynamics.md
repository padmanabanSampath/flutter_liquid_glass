## Q1. Observed dynamics in looped LLMs and looped ViTs: convergence, orbits/cycles, sliders/drift, and whether per-token states settle

### Takeaway
Most tokens in trained looped LMs converge toward a fixed point as passes increase. A minority do something else. Huginn's PCA plots show "orbits" and "sliders" on arithmetic- and deliberation-type tokens. Ouro drifts slowly without reaching a strict fixed point inside typical loop counts. Multi-block cycles (A→B→A→B) settle into *cyclic* fixed points: each block position has its own point, so per-block differences never go to zero. Convergence is uneven across tokens: content words and hard tokens settle later. Answers can stop changing while scores keep moving.

### Cited Findings
**Huginn / recurrent-depth (Geiping et al., arXiv 2502.05171, Feb 2025)**
- The architecture has a prelude, a shared recurrent core block and a coda. The input embedding is re-injected at every step, the latent state starts from random noise, and the number of training iterations is sampled at random (described as a log-normal Poisson distribution) [secondary] — [Emergent Mind: Huginn-3.5B](https://www.emergentmind.com/topics/huginn-3-5b)
- The paper plots token trajectories on the first 6 PCA directions. Wording: "While on many tokens, the state simply converges (top row), the model also learns to use orbits (middle row), and 'sliders' (bottom row, middle) to represent and handle more advanced concepts, such as arithmetic or complicated deliberation." The slider example the search summary gives is the token "wrong" [search excerpt] — [Geiping et al. 2025 PDF](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2502.05171v1)
- Blogs describe orbits as rotations on numeric tasks and sliders as steady drift in "moral reasoning". That reading goes beyond the paper, which ties these patterns to arithmetic and deliberation [secondary] — [Medium: The Silent Mind of AI](https://medium.com/@sergiosear/the-silent-mind-of-ai-exploring-huginn-4a797bd62cf5)
- Zero-shot adaptive exit: the run stops when the KL divergence between successive steps falls below **5 × 10⁻⁴**. Per-token exit histograms on MMLU show the model converging faster on high-school mathematics than on logical fallacies or moral scenarios [search excerpt] — [Geiping et al. 2025](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2502.05171)
- The HF model card lists the stopping criteria `entropy-diff`, `latent-diff`, `kl` and `argmax-stability`, with a default `exit_threshold` of 5e-4, and says the model is *not trained* for early exit (a zero-shot proof of concept). `continuous_compute=True` warm-starts each token's recurrence from the previous token's final state [search excerpt] — [huginn-0125 model card](https://huggingface.co/tomg-group-umd/huginn-0125)
- The official evaluation commands run Huginn at `mean_recurrence=32` [primary] — [seal-rg/recurrent-pretraining README](https://github.com/seal-rg/recurrent-pretraining)
- Skeptical reading (Lu et al., arXiv 2507.02199, Jul 2025, COLM 2025 workshop): tracing the rank dynamics of intermediate and final answer tokens shows "no clear temporal separation or structured latent reasoning pathway across recurrence steps", and probing is inconsistent across blocks [search excerpt] — [Lu et al. 2025](https://arxiv.org/html/2507.02199)

**Mechanistic analysis of looped LMs (Blayney et al., arXiv 2604.11791, Apr 2026)**
- Many looped models do not converge to one static state across all layers. They reach "cyclic fixed points": the output of the whole block stabilizes while each layer settles at its own point. Proposition 4.1: if an (l,k)-recurrent block reaches a fixed point S_k(X′)=X′, then every cyclic permutation of blocks 1…k also reaches a fixed point [search excerpt] — [Blayney et al. 2026](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2604.11791)
- As these fixed points are reached, attention-head behavior stabilizes and stays constant across recurrences (attention matrices lock into a repeating pattern; Fig. 2 compares Frobenius-norm similarity of attention matrices across depths). The recurrent blocks learn "stages of inference" that mirror those of feedforward models and repeat them in depth [search excerpt] — [Blayney et al. 2026](https://www.alphaxiv.org/abs/2604.11791)
- Huginn-0125 and retrofitted Llama reach a fixed point quickly. Ouro-1.4B does not, "despite small successive differences": it drifts slowly and reaches no strict fixed point within typical recurrence counts. Running Ouro past its training recurrences harms performance [search excerpt] — [Blayney et al. 2026](https://arxiv.org/html/2604.11791v1)
- With input injection, every norm type except Ouro's reaches a stable fixed point. Without input injection, only pre-norm does [search excerpt] — [Blayney et al. 2026](https://arxiv.org/html/2604.11791v1)
- The paper covers cyclic recurrence only. Its findings do not automatically transfer to "sequential recurrence" models that chain several separate recurrent blocks [secondary] — [ArXivIQ summary](https://arxiviq.substack.com/p/a-mechanistic-analysis-of-looped)

**Per-token settling (2025–2026)**
- *Per-Token Fixed-Point Convergence in Depth-Recurrent Transformers* (Logan, arXiv 2607.14427, Jul 2026; 135M params, FineWeb-Edu). Mean output divergence between successive loops falls from ≈**0.39 at loop 2 to ≈8.5e-6 at loop 16**. The median token converges by **loop 6**, while ≈**10% of tokens are still updating at the training-mean depth of 8**. Whitespace tokens settle first and content words last. A training-free per-token rule that stops once the output stabilizes matches uniform depth-8 quality at **4.94 average loops (−38%)**; a learned linear router achieved no reduction. Caveats: depth is a FLOP proxy, not measured speed, and results come from one scale and one seed [search excerpt] — [Logan 2026](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.14427); same caveats noted by [Pith review](https://pith.science/paper/2607.14427) [secondary]
- *Two-Scale Latent Dynamics for Recurrent-Depth Transformers* (Pappone, Crisostomi, Rodolà, arXiv 2509.23314, Sep 2025, NeurIPS 2025 workshop). Over training, loop steps get **smaller and increasingly orthogonal to one another**: fine local refinement inside a block, larger drift across blocks. A second-order exit rule (on the change in step size) beats Geiping's KL exit and its first-order variant on performance, stability and time-efficiency [search excerpt] — [Pappone et al. 2025](https://arxiv.org/abs/2509.23314); [NeurIPS workshop page](https://neurips.cc/virtual/2025/loc/san-diego/127222)
- *Prediction Dynamics in Depth-Recurrent Language Models* (Luo & Yu, arXiv 2609.21383, Sep 2026; Huginn-3.5B and Ouro-1.4B). Intermediate answers can match the final answer while their scores keep shifting. "Common" and "contrast" update energies shrink at different rates, so the share of score change that comes from preference shifts can *grow* even as absolute updates shrink. Modeling update direction and competitor pairing cuts the earliest qualifying (safe-to-stop) depth by a further **22.5–34.4% of total depth** (retrospective, on completed trajectories) [search excerpt] — [Luo & Yu 2026](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.21383)
- *LayerNorm as Implicit Gain Control in Looped Transformers* (Buehlmaier, arXiv 2607.10681, Jul 2026). LayerNorm inside the loop lowers the block's effective gain as activations grow, which makes the recurrence Jacobian non-normal. Stability is set by the *spectral margin*, not by an operator-norm bound. The margin shrinks as the diagonal carry approaches 1, and **some random initializations never reach a fixed point**. Convergence toward a fixed point is spiral-shaped [search excerpt + secondary] — [Buehlmaier 2026](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2607.10681); [Pith](https://pith.science/paper/2607.10681)
- *Fixed-Point Reasoners* (Movahedi et al., arXiv 2606.18206, Jun 2026): "For some inputs, the model often descends into an oscillatory behavior, causing the iteration to stay in a small region of latent space without converging." They attribute this to a Jacobian eigenvalue with Re(λ) < 1 but |λ| ≥ 1 [search excerpt] — [Movahedi et al. 2026](https://arxiv.org/html/2606.18206v1)
- A 2026 paper (summarized by search) says recurrent states in looped LMs approach fixed points as depth grows, and that in Huginn later tokens can reach approximate fixed points before earlier ones [search excerpt] — [Towards Looped Models Done Right Part II](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2610.06833)

**Ouro (LoopLM, arXiv 2510.25741, Oct 2025)**
- Ouro 1.4B was trained with at most **4** recurrent steps. Table 10 labels steps 5–8 as extrapolation. Performance peaks at or near the trained depth and declines beyond it. The project page describes the drop as slight and lists a T=8 extrapolated average of 76.44 (model size not clear from the snippet) [search excerpt/secondary] — [Ouro project page](https://ouro-llm.github.io/); [ResearchGate copy](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/397040734_Scaling_Latent_Reasoning_via_Looped_Language_Models)

**Older algorithmic-task evidence**
- Looped transformers for in-context learning (Yang et al., ICLR 2024): "While trained for 30 loop iterations, the looped transformer during inference achieves a stable fixed-point solution beyond the trained loop iterations" [search excerpt] — [Yang et al. ICLR 2024](https://proceedings.iclr.cc/paper_files/paper/2024/file/b8402301e7f06bdc97a31bfaa653dc32-Paper-Conference.pdf)

**Vision (looped / block-recurrent ViTs)**
- *Block-Recurrent Dynamics in Vision Transformers* (Jacobs, Fel, Hakim, Brondetta, Ba, Keller; arXiv 2512.19941, Dec 2025, revised 2026). The "Block-Recurrent Hypothesis" says the L blocks of a trained ViT can be rewritten as k≪L distinct blocks applied recurrently. Their "Raptor" surrogate takes its blocks from a max-cut partition of layer similarities and, on **DINOv2, 2–3 blocks recover ≈96–98% of linear-probe accuracy**. Under "dynamical interpretability", depth acts as a discrete dynamical system in which **token directions converge to angular attractors** and late-depth updates are low-rank and coherent [secondary] — [alphaXiv overview](https://www.alphaxiv.org/overview/2512.19941v5); [Emergent Mind](https://www.emergentmind.com/papers/2512.19941)
- RD-ViT (arXiv 2605.03999, 2026) adds depth-wise LoRA because "pure weight sharing may be suboptimal because early and late iterations serve different functional roles". Déjà View (arXiv 2605.30215) finds that shared weights with time-conditioned gates beat decoupled per-step blocks with 16× fewer parameters. ELT (arXiv 2604.09168) supervises intermediate loops in visual generation [search excerpt] — [RD-ViT](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2605.03999); [Déjà View](https://arxiv.org/html/2605.30215); [ELT](https://arxiv.org/html/2604.09168v2)

### Inferences
- For an A→B→A→B student, the convergence object is the composite map F = B∘A (a period-2 cycle at the block level). By Blayney's Prop. 4.1, if x* = B(A(x*)) then A(x*) is a fixed point of A∘B. Diagnostics should compare states **one full cycle apart** (h after A-B vs h after the next A-B). Comparing consecutive blocks will never show zero change, and that is expected, not a failure.
- The cube analogy maps onto dynamics directly. A permutation (finite bijection) has only periodic orbits, which is what the Huginn "orbit" tokens look like. A trained looped LM is mostly a contraction toward per-token attractors, with a minority of tokens on orbits or slow drift (sliders, Ouro). "Later passes change less" is the majority behavior but not guaranteed, and it is weakest on hard or content tokens. Those are exactly the tokens where you want more passes.
- Since ≈10% of tokens are still moving at the trained depth (Logan 2026) and answer scores keep shifting after the argmax settles (Luo & Yu 2026), per-token early exit should be judged on the **output distribution** (KL / argmax / margin), not only on hidden-state norm.

## Q2. Deep Equilibrium Models and stability: Jacobian regularization, monotone operator DEQs, contraction via spectral norm / Lipschitz constraints, and the quality cost

### Takeaway
DEQs solve z* = f(z*, x) directly with a root solver and train in O(1) memory by implicit differentiation. Unregularized DEQs get less stable during training (NFE creeps up). Penalizing the Jacobian Frobenius norm (Bai et al. 2021) roughly halves to thirds the solver iterations at a small quality cost (WikiText-103 PPL ≈24.0 → 24.5–24.9; ImageNet 77.5% → 76.8%). Hard guarantees (monotone operator DEQs, spectral-norm contractions) give a unique fixed point but have historically cost accuracy or capacity.

### Cited Findings
- DEQ (Bai, Kolter, Koltun, NeurIPS 2019): training and prediction "require only constant memory, regardless of the effective 'depth' of the network" [search excerpt] — [Bai et al. 2019](https://arxiv.org/abs/1909.01377)
- The official DEQ repo provides Anderson and Broyden solvers and, "by default, we use the *relative residual difference* (i.e., |f(z)-z|/|z|) as the criterion for stopping". The backward pass solves the linear fixed point of yJ + grad at z* [primary] — [locuslab/deq README](https://github.com/locuslab/deq)
- Repo flags for Jacobian regularization: `jac_loss_weight` (regularizes ‖J_f‖_F), `jac_loss_freq` (apply the loss with probability p), and `jac_incremental` (raise the weight by 0.1 every N steps). `lib/jacobian.py` implements a Hutchinson estimator (`jac_loss_estimate(f0, z0, vecs=2)`, which sums ‖vJ‖² over random vectors) and a power method for the spectral radius (`power_method(f0, z0, n_iters=200)`). Solvers also return `rel_trace`, the relative residuals along the trajectory [primary] — [locuslab/deq README](https://github.com/locuslab/deq); [lib/jacobian.py](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/locuslab/deq/master/lib/jacobian.py)
- *Stabilizing Equilibrium Models by Jacobian Regularization* (Bai, Koltun, Kolter, ICML 2021, arXiv 2106.14342):
  - WikiText-103 DEQ-Transformer (98M): NFE drops from ≈**30 to 12–14**, PPL ≈**24.0 unregularized vs 24.5–24.9 regularized**. The post-LN regularized model reaches 24.9 PPL at 12 NFEs, while an early-stopped unregularized model reaches only **29.2 PPL at 12 NFEs** [search excerpt/secondary] — [Bai et al. 2021](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2106.14342); [Liner review](https://liner.com/review/stabilizing-equilibrium-models-by-jacobian-regularization)
  - CIFAR-10 MDEQ: ≈**6 NFEs vs 17**, ≈2× speedup, 93.1% accuracy. ImageNet MDEQ-large: NFE **27–30 → 14–15**, accuracy **77.5% → 76.8%**; the paper notes the reduction is weaker at ImageNet scale. ‖J‖_F serves as a tractable proxy for the spectral radius [secondary] — [alphaXiv overview](https://www.alphaxiv.org/overview/2106.14342v1)
- *Monotone operator equilibrium networks* (Winston & Kolter, NeurIPS 2020, arXiv 2006.08591). A weight parameterization keeps the operator monotone, which **guarantees a unique equilibrium**. The equilibrium is found with operator-splitting methods (forward-backward, Peaceman-Rachford) that have guaranteed linear convergence; naive forward iteration is not guaranteed stable [search excerpt] — [Winston & Kolter 2020](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2006.08591)
- monDEQ CIFAR-10 accuracies as cited by a later paper: **74.0%** (monDEQ single-conv, ≈172K params) and **72.0%** (multi-tier, ≈170K), or **82.0% / 89.0%** with data augmentation at ≈854K / 1M params. These sit well below the ≈93% of unconstrained MDEQ, which illustrates the cost of hard guarantees [secondary; not checked against the original] — [Positive Concave DEQ, arXiv 2402.04029](https://arxiv.org/html/2402.04029)
- *Rethinking Deep Thinking: Stable Learning of Algorithms using Lipschitz Constraints* (Bear, Prügel-Bennett, Hare, NeurIPS 2024, arXiv 2410.23451). Spectral norms of trained Deep-Thinking conv layers are typically **>1**, so the state can grow every iteration and overflow on large inputs. DT-L uses spectral normalization to keep the recurrent step's Lipschitz constant slightly below 1 (a contraction, so convergence to a unique fixed point is guaranteed), plus 1-Lipschitz activations (ELU), learned linear-interpolation residuals, and separate input convolutions. It reports ≈**3 orders of magnitude fewer parameters than DT-R** with high accuracy, more consistent training, better extrapolation, and the ability to learn TSP where the original DT fails [search excerpt] — [Bear et al. 2024](https://arxiv.org/html/2410.23451); [NeurIPS poster](https://neurips.cc/virtual/2024/poster/92936)
- Standard dot-product self-attention is **not globally Lipschitz** (Kim, Papamakarios, Mnih, ICML 2021). Spectral normalization of the weights alone therefore cannot make a transformer block a guaranteed contraction; L2 attention is the Lipschitz alternative [background] — [The Lipschitz Constant of Self-Attention](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2006.04710)
- Reversible DEQ (McCallum, Arora, Foster, arXiv 2509.12917, Sep 2025): standard DEQ gradients approximate the implicit function theorem and are inexact, which "causes training instability and oftentimes training diverges altogether". RevDEQ uses an algebraically reversible fixed-point solver to get exact gradients at constant memory, and needs far fewer function evaluations than DEQs [search excerpt] — [McCallum et al. 2025](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2509.12917)
- *Solve the Loop: Attractor Models for Language and Reasoning* (Fein-Ashley & Rashidinejad, arXiv 2605.12466, May 2026, ICML 2026). A causal backbone proposes an output, a smaller weight-tied "attractor" module refines it, and a root finder stops at a residual tolerance or a maximum step count. Training uses implicit differentiation, so memory does not grow with iterations. **"Equilibrium internalization"**: during training the backbone's first proposal moves toward the fixed point, so the solver can be dropped at inference with little loss. Claimed gains: up to **46.6% perplexity improvement** and up to **19.7% downstream accuracy** over standard transformers and stable looped models [search excerpt] — [Solve the Loop](https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.12466); [ICML 2026 page](https://icml.cc/virtual/2026/70473)

### Inferences
- For a deployed looped student, contraction buys three things: a unique fixed point (warm starts are safe), geometric decay of ‖h_{k+1}−h_k‖ (early exit), and robustness to extra passes. The DEQ literature prices this at a few percent of quality when imposed softly (Jacobian penalty) and much more when imposed as a hard architectural guarantee (monDEQ, ≈74–89% vs ≈93% on CIFAR-10). A soft penalty applied stochastically (`jac_loss_freq`) is the practical middle ground.
- By the Banach fixed-point theorem, if the per-cycle map has Lipschitz constant L<1 then ‖h_k − h*‖ ≤ L/(1−L) · ‖h_k − h_{k−1}‖. Measuring L (power method) together with the update norm therefore gives a certified distance-to-fixed-point for early exit. This is standard math, not a result from these papers.
- "Equilibrium internalization" (Solve the Loop) is the DEQ analogue of distilling a loop into fewer passes. It suggests training the first pass to land near the fixed point, which makes warm starts and early exit cheaper.

### Gaps
- Could not verify the exact Bai 2021 table values against the PDF (arXiv blocked), nor the default relative-residual tolerance (eps) used in DEQ solvers.
- monDEQ accuracy numbers come only from a citing paper.
- Found no published Jacobian spectral-radius values for trained looped LLMs (Huginn, Ouro) apart from STARS's regularizer (Q6), which is described qualitatively only.

## Q3. Path independence (Anil et al., NeurIPS 2022), warm starts, test-time extrapolation, and overthinking / recall in Deep Thinking networks

### Takeaway
A looped model is *path independent* when it reaches the same asymptotic behavior (a fixed point or limit cycle) from any initial state. That property, measured by the Asymptotic Alignment (AA) score, predicts whether extra test-time iterations help (accuracy rises monotonically) or hurt (path-dependent models degrade past the training depth). Path independence also makes warm starts safe. Input injection or "recall", randomized initial states and randomized iteration counts all promote it. Deep Thinking nets without recall "overthink": they fall to 0% at large sizes, against >97% with recall plus progressive loss.

### Cited Findings
- Definition: an equilibrium model is path independent if the solver "lands on the same asymptotic behaviour regardless of initialization", where asymptotic behaviour means "a fixed point or limit cycle" [search excerpt] — [Anil et al. 2022](https://arxiv.org/abs/2211.09961); authors Cem Anil, Ashwini Pokle, Kaiqu Liang, Johannes Treutlein, Yuhuai Wu, Shaojie Bai, J. Zico Kolter, Roger Grosse — [OpenReview](https://openreview.net/forum?id=kgT6D7Z4Xv9)
- AA score: the average cosine similarity between fixed points reached from the training-time initialization and fixed points reached when the solver starts from states computed on *other* inputs. Score 1 means fully path independent. It is cheap, unitless and comparable across training runs [search excerpt] — [Anil et al. 2022](https://arxiv.org/html/2211.09961)
- The AA score "is strongly correlated" with accuracy when far more iterations are used at test time than at training time. Path-independent models improve monotonically with test-time iterations; path-dependent models degrade once iterations exceed the training count. Tasks: sequence prediction, visual reasoning, image classification, continuous optimization, graph regression [search excerpt] — [Anil et al. 2022](https://arxiv.org/html/2211.09961)
- Stress test: on 13×13 mazes the PI network scores **AA 1.00 / 100% accuracy** and the non-PI network **AA 0.32 / 87.12%**. On 64-bit prefix sum: **0.99 / 100%** vs **0.62 / 66.66%**. Non-PI networks "can be easily steered away from the initial fixed point estimate through adversarial initializations", PI networks with high AA cannot [search excerpt] — [Anil et al. 2022 PDF](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2211.09961)
- "Input injection and implicit updates improve both the path independence and the accuracy" (abstract) [search excerpt] — [Anil et al. 2022](https://arxiv.org/abs/2211.09961)
- Follow-up descriptions of the PI training recipe: random initialization for half the batch and zero initialization for the other half, and varying the forward solver's compute budget or depth during training. Randomized initialization "can lead the model to learn path independence" [search excerpt, from citing papers] — [search result summary of Anil et al. follow-ups](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2211.09961)
- Caveat: AA is a proxy and does not prove *global* path independence [secondary] — [microprediction/latentreasoning issue #159](https://github.com/microprediction/latentreasoning/issues/159)
- Replication caveat (arXiv 2410.03020, 2024): pretrained maze models from prior work generalize as maze *size* grows but **not** when dead-end start and percolation p are varied [search excerpt] — [On Logical Extrapolation for Mazes](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2410.03020)
- Overthinking and recall (Bansal et al., NeurIPS 2022, arXiv 2202.05826). Deep Thinking nets run far beyond their training regime "deteriorate and fail to produce interpretable outputs". The fix: **recall** (concatenating the original input into the recurrent module at every step) and **progressive loss** (run a random number of no-grad iterations first, then train k more steps from that state, so the model learns behaviors that can be repeated indefinitely). Without these, networks reach **0% on 512-bit prefix sums**. DT-Recall with progressive loss solves **>97% after ≈200 iterations**, and both pieces are needed [search excerpt] — [Bansal et al. 2022](https://proceedings.neurips.cc/paper_files/paper/2022/file/7f70331dbe58ad59d83941dfa7d975aa-Paper-Conference.pdf); reproducibility study confirms recall plus progressive loss "prevent the original problem being forgotten or corrupted" [search excerpt] — [[Re] study](https://openreview.net/pdf/07d5d68b5873d779bd1fd8c95b9767cb57fe0bc4.pdf)
- The official deep-thinking repo ships "architectures with recall and a training routine with the progressive loss term". The prefix-sums default config sets `train_mode: progressive`, `alpha: 1`, `clip: 1`, `lr: 0.001`, 150 epochs [primary] — [aks2203/deep-thinking](https://github.com/aks2203/deep-thinking)
- *Learning to Stop Overthinking at Test Time* (arXiv 2502.10954, Feb 2025): DT models cannot judge sample difficulty, so they spend large compute even on easy inputs, and "more test time computation leads to worse results". The paper proposes test-time training with a self-supervised task to estimate the needed compute, plus a Conv-LiGRU recurrent cell that is more stable than DT [search excerpt] — [Tran Bao et al. 2025](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2502.10954)
- Huginn adopts the same ideas at LLM scale: the input is re-injected each step and the latent state starts at random, explicitly "for path independence" [secondary] — [Emergent Mind](https://www.emergentmind.com/topics/huginn-3-5b)
- Warm starting across frames (DEQ-Flow, Bai, Geng, Savani, Kolter, CVPR 2022): the previous frame's fixed point initializes the next frame's solver because consecutive frames are highly correlated. "Only frame 0 requires full fixed-point solving", and the rest recycle their predecessor's flow. RAFT's warm start differs: it covers only the flow f, not the hidden state h, and still unrolls many steps [search excerpt] — [Bai et al. CVPR 2022](https://openaccess.thecvf.com/content/CVPR2022/papers/Bai_Deep_Equilibrium_Optical_Flow_Estimation_CVPR_2022_paper.pdf); [locuslab/deq-flow](https://github.com/locuslab/deq-flow) [primary: repo exists and matches paper]
- *Towards Looped Models Done Right, Part II: Rethinking at Fixed Points* (Huang, Shi, Chen, Wen, Liu, Xing, Ma; arXiv 2610.06833, Sep/Oct 2026). States near a fixed point depend less on the path taken, and the paper uses this for truncated backprop, **terminal KV sharing** during decoding, a distilled student with faster prefill, and RL gradients from saved rollout states. A 1.6B looped model runs **12 blocks deep on the memory of 4**, beats a 4-block transformer by **17.5%**, and comes within **2.4%** of a 12-block transformer that needs 3× the memory. A **3× smaller KV cache** matches full-cache fixed-depth training on the downstream average. The depth prior is learned with an entropy term, and input injection is orthogonal. Code: github.com/ifm-ai/xllm-loop [search excerpt] — [Huang et al. 2026](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.06833)
- *Stabilizing Extrapolation in Looped Transformers via Learned Stochastic Stopping* (Kuo, Chayti, Reizinger, Brendel, Jaggi; arXiv 2606.29983, 2026). On simple algorithmic tasks, run-to-run variance comes from a spurious correlation between sequence length and loop count. Stochastic loop schedules "substantially reduce run-to-run OOD variability and make predictions more stable across inference-time loop counts". Learned RL-Halting improves the accuracy-stability trade-off but on Copy "can sometimes stabilize a suboptimal strategy" [search excerpt] — [Kuo et al. 2026](https://arxiv.org/html/2606.29983v1)

### Inferences
- Warm starts in the user's deployments come in three kinds: per-frame vision (previous frame's state), token models (previous token's final state, Huginn `continuous_compute`), and KV sharing (Part II). Each is safe only to the extent the student is path independent. A cheap acceptance test: compute an AA-style score on the student by initializing from (a) the trained init, (b) a previous frame's or token's state, and (c) random noise, then compare final states and outputs by cosine similarity and KL. A low AA predicts both warm-start errors and degradation when running more passes on hard inputs.
- Distillation from a fixed-depth teacher at a fixed loop count invites path dependence, as with Ouro's drift and degradation past its trained T=4. Randomizing the student's loop count and initial state during distillation is the cheapest documented remedy.

### Gaps
- Exact Anil et al. ablation numbers (AA with and without input injection, correlation coefficients) were not retrievable; arXiv and NeurIPS were blocked.
- DEQ-Flow's numeric iteration savings from fixed-point reuse were not retrieved.
- Found no study measuring AA scores on looped *LLMs* (Huginn, Ouro).

## Q4. Reversible / invertible blocks (RevNet, Reformer, i-RevNet, i-ResNet): the neural analogue of invertible cube moves, memory benefits, and whether they can converge

### Takeaway
Reversible blocks make activations reconstructible, so training memory does not grow with depth or loops. That is attractive for training deep loops. The common additive-coupling form (RevNet, Reformer) is **volume-preserving** (Jacobian determinant 1), so it cannot have an attracting fixed point. Like a cube move, it can only rotate, shear or cycle, never settle. Invertible residual blocks of the form x + g(x) with Lip(g)<1 *can* contract in some directions. The cleanest way to combine "reversible for memory" with "convergent for inference" is a reversible *solver* (RevDEQ) or reversible training around a contractive map, not a reversible block that is itself the iterated map.

### Cited Findings
- RevNet (Gomez et al., NeurIPS 2017): "each layer's activations can be reconstructed exactly from the next layer's. Therefore, the activations for most layers need not be stored in memory during backpropagation." Only non-reversible layers such as subsampling need explicit storage [search excerpt] — [Gomez et al. 2017](https://arxiv.org/abs/1707.04585); [review](https://sh-tsang.medium.com/review-the-reversible-residual-network-backpropagation-without-storing-activations-526e6d5c60f4) [secondary]
- Reformer (Kitaev et al., ICLR 2020) applies this to transformers with Y1 = X1 + Attention(X2), Y2 = X2 + FeedForward(Y1), and states the memory savings come "without accuracy" loss [search excerpt] — [Reformer](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2001.04451)
- Invertible ResNets (Behrmann et al., ICML 2019): a Lipschitz constant <1 for each residual block g is *sufficient* (not necessary) for x ↦ x + g(x) to be invertible, which permits unconstrained block architectures. The inverse is computed by fixed-point iteration (from any start, since the fixed point is unique; the output y is a good start), with geometric convergence that is faster for smaller Lipschitz constants. The constraint is enforced by spectral normalization, scaling each W by c/‖W‖₂ when ‖W‖₂ > c, with ‖W‖₂ estimated by power iteration [search excerpt] — [Behrmann et al. 2019](https://proceedings.mlr.press/v97/behrmann19a/behrmann19a.pdf)
- RevDEQ (arXiv 2509.12917, 2025): an algebraically reversible fixed-point *solver* gives exact gradients with memory constant in effective depth, needs far fewer function evaluations than DEQs, and improves language-modeling and image-classification results over comparable implicit and explicit models [search excerpt] — [McCallum et al. 2025](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2509.12917)
- Huginn's code base includes a reversible-style block variant (`y1 = attn(norm_1(x2)) + x1; y2 = mlp(norm_2(y1)) + x2`) alongside pre-norm, post-norm and sandwich variants [primary] — [recpre/model_dynamic.py](https://github.com/seal-rg/recurrent-pretraining/blob/main/recpre/model_dynamic.py)

### Inferences
- These points are mathematical reasoning, not results from the cited papers:
  - An additive coupling map has Jacobian determinant exactly 1, so the product of its eigenvalue magnitudes is 1, and it cannot have all |λ|<1 at any point. It therefore has no asymptotically stable fixed point and cannot make "later passes change less" in all directions. This is the precise neural analogue of the cube: invertible and volume-preserving means orbits recur and never settle, and on a finite state space a bijection is a permutation whose orbits are all periodic.
  - Invertibility alone does not forbid convergence: x ↦ x/2 is invertible and globally contracting, and an i-ResNet block x+g(x) has Jacobian I+J_g whose eigenvalues lie within distance Lip(g)<1 of 1, so their magnitudes can fall below 1.
  - A contraction forgets its initial state, which is what path independence requires. In an input-injected loop h_{k+1}=f(h_k, x), information about x survives through re-injection, not through invertibility of the state map.
- Practical trade-off for the user: reversible blocks cut training memory for deep or long loops (useful when distilling a many-pass student), but a reversible block used as the iterated map works against early exit and warm starts. Options: (a) train with reversible or recompute memory tricks but keep the deployed block contractive (Jacobian penalty or damping); (b) use a contractive i-ResNet-style block; (c) use a RevDEQ-style reversible solver.

### Gaps
- Found no source analyzing i-RevNet (Jacobsen et al. 2018) dynamics, and no paper on reversible *looped transformers* as iterated maps. The volume-preservation argument above is my own reasoning, not a cited result.
- Found no measurements of quality loss from making a looped LM block reversible.

## Q5. Practical diagnostics for a trained looped student: update norms, Jacobian spectral radius, KL between successive passes, cycle detection, thresholds

### Takeaway
The tools in use are: the relative residual ‖f(h)−h‖/‖h‖ (the DEQ default stop criterion; also logged in Huginn training as `rel_residual`), KL between successive pass outputs (Huginn exit at 5e-4), argmax stability or entropy change, second-order step-size change (Two-Scale), Hutchinson ‖J‖_F and power-method spectral radius (DEQ repo, 200 iterations), AA score for path independence, and token-correlation monitoring to catch collapse. Published thresholds are scarce. The only concrete one found is KL < 5×10⁻⁴.

### Cited Findings
- Huginn's training code logs, per step: `recurrent_residual` = mean‖x_rec − x_k‖, `rel_residual` = mean(‖x_rec − x_k‖/‖x_rec‖), `recurrent_diff` = ‖x_rec − input_embeds‖, state norms, and the mean pairwise token cosine correlation of the recurrent state (`recurrent_state_token_corr`) [primary] — [recpre/model_dynamic.py monitor_module](https://github.com/seal-rg/recurrent-pretraining/blob/main/recpre/model_dynamic.py)
- Huginn inference exit criteria: `entropy-diff`, `latent-diff`, `kl` (default threshold 5e-4) and `argmax-stability` [search excerpt] — [huginn-0125 model card](https://huggingface.co/tomg-group-umd/huginn-0125)
- DEQ stopping uses the relative residual |f(z)−z|/|z|. Solvers return `rel_trace` (residual along the trajectory). `lib/jacobian.py` provides a Hutchinson ‖J‖_F estimate (2 random vectors by default) and a power method for the dominant eigenvalue (n_iters=200) [primary] — [locuslab/deq](https://github.com/locuslab/deq); [lib/jacobian.py](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/locuslab/deq/master/lib/jacobian.py)
- Second-order exit (change in step size) beats KL and first-order exits in performance, stability and time-efficiency [search excerpt] — [Pappone et al. 2025](https://arxiv.org/abs/2509.23314)
- Per-token output stabilization as a training-free halting rule: depth-8 quality at 4.94 average loops. Successive-loop output divergence ≈0.39 at loop 2 and ≈8.5e-6 at loop 16 [search excerpt] — [Logan 2026](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.14427)
- Margin-based stopping that models update direction and competitor pairing cuts the earliest safe depth by 22.5–34.4% of total depth beyond magnitude bounds [search excerpt] — [Luo & Yu 2026](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.21383)
- Attention-matrix similarity across recurrences (Frobenius norm) as a sign of a reached (cyclic) fixed point [search excerpt] — [Blayney et al. 2026](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2604.11791)
- Oscillation diagnosis: an eigenvalue of J with Re(λ)<1 and |λ|≥1 signals oscillation that damping can cure [search excerpt] — [Movahedi et al. 2026](https://arxiv.org/html/2606.18206v1)
- Collapse diagnosis: Huginn's first failed large run showed hidden-state token correlation rising to **1.0** (the same state for every token). The second run's validation perplexity was **identical at 1 and 32 recurrences**, meaning the model had learned to ignore its state [search excerpt] — [Geiping et al. 2025](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2502.05171)
- AA score (cosine similarity of fixed points across initializations) for path independence [search excerpt] — [Anil et al. 2022](https://arxiv.org/html/2211.09961)

### Inferences
Suggested diagnostic panel for an A→B→A→B student, composed from the tools above:
1. Per-cycle relative residual r_k = ‖h_{k+2}−h_k‖/‖h_k‖ (one A-B cycle apart), per token or patch, plotted against k. Healthy: geometric decay. The slope of log r_k estimates the contraction factor L.
2. Successive-cycle output KL and argmax flips. Exit when KL < ~5e-4 (Huginn's zero-shot value; tune per model).
3. Power-method spectral radius of the per-cycle Jacobian at the final state on a few hundred samples. Flag samples with ρ ≥ 1, and separately flag complex eigenvalues with Re<1 and |λ|≥1 (oscillation that damping can fix).
4. Cycle detection: for tokens whose r_k plateaus above tolerance, test whether ‖h_{k+p}−h_k‖ is small for some p>2 (period-p orbit) versus a monotone drift in one direction (slider).
5. AA score from trained, warm and random inits.
6. Token-correlation monitor to catch collapse during distillation.

### Gaps
- Found no published practical thresholds for relative residual or spectral radius in looped LLM deployment, other than Huginn's KL 5e-4.
- Found no published cycle-detection procedure for looped LMs; Huginn's orbit observations are visual (PCA).

## Q6. Training levers that promote convergence: random iteration counts, per-pass losses, damping/relaxation, normalization placement, residual scaling, input re-injection, and their measured effects

### Takeaway
The levers with measured stability effects are:
- **Input re-injection** (recall), needed for stable fixed points except with pre-norm.
- **Randomized loop counts and initial states** (Huginn, progressive loss, stochastic stopping), which reduce variance and make models robust to extra passes.
- **Normalization placement**: pre-norm risks exponential state growth, post-norm stays bounded but reasons poorly, and Huginn needed sandwich norm plus a 10× lower LR to avoid collapse.
- **Residual scaling of 1/N in the loop count**.
- **Damping** h ← ηf(h)+(1−η)h, which removes oscillations.
- **Explicit Jacobian or spectral-radius regularization** (DEQ, STARS).

### Cited Findings
**Normalization placement**
- Huginn's first large run (sandwich blocks with parameter-free RMSNorm, no embedding scale, parameter-free adapter, peak LR 4×10⁻⁴) collapsed: token correlation went to 1.0, with each recurrence raising correlation further. The second run (embedding scale, pre-norm, learned adapter) recovered after ≈150 steps but ignored the recurrent state (same perplexity at 1 and 32 recurrences). The final run returned to sandwich norm with peak LR **4×10⁻⁵** [search excerpt] — [Geiping et al. 2025](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2502.05171); also recounted in [Closing the Loop, arXiv 2610.00673](https://arxiv.org/html/2610.00673v1)
- Huginn's code implements pre-norm, post-norm, sandwich (norm before and after each sublayer) and residual/skip-scaled variants (`x = attn(norm(x))*res_scale + x*skip_scale`) [primary] — [recpre/model_dynamic.py](https://github.com/seal-rg/recurrent-pretraining/blob/main/recpre/model_dynamic.py)
- STARS (Yang et al., arXiv 2605.26733, May 2026, ICML 2026): looped LM performance "often peaks at a certain iteration depth and deteriorates sharply or even collapses entirely as iterations further increase". Pre-norm "causes hidden states to grow exponentially, leading to trajectory divergence"; post-norm "keeps states bounded but often fails to perform deep reasoning". STARS constrains states toward asymptotically stable fixed points via **Jacobian spectral-radius regularization with random loop sampling**, mitigating the depth degradation and improving peak math-reasoning performance [search excerpt] — [Yang et al. 2026](https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.26733)
- Without input injection, only pre-norm loops reach a stable fixed point. With injection, all norm types except Ouro's do [search excerpt] — [Blayney et al. 2026](https://arxiv.org/html/2604.11791v1)
- LayerNorm acts as implicit gain control. The resulting non-normal Jacobian means operator-norm bounds mis-predict stability; the spectral margin governs it [search excerpt] — [Buehlmaier 2026](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2607.10681)

**Residual scaling and architecture**
- Residual scaling (Wang et al., arXiv 2606.18524, Jun 2026; ICLR 2026 LIT workshop). Weight sharing correlates residual updates across iterations, so 1/√N scaling is insufficient and **ε = 1/N** (N = loop count) is needed. For multi-layer blocks ε = λ/(N√L). With this scaling the optimal LR depends only on the number of unique layers, which allows hyperparameter transfer across loop counts. 1/N yields better loss and trainability than 1/√N [search excerpt] — [Wang et al. 2026](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.18524)
- Fully Looped Transformer (arXiv 2605.18797, May 2026). Looped transformers become unstable as loop count grows, from **gradient oscillation and residual explosion**. Distributing inter-loop signals across all layers plus "Attention Injection" trains stably up to **12 loops** where baselines collapse, and adds up to **+13.2%** downstream in milder settings [search excerpt] — [FLT](https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.18797)
- A Lacuna summary reports that "models trained with 8 loops often break when run with 48" (different setting, not FLT's result) [secondary] — [Lacuna](https://lacuna.tiptreesystems.com/work/simply-stabilizing-the-loop-via-fully-looped-transformer/wrk_3558d77d371d0ef526de76f02c6d46d5)

**Damping / relaxation**
- Fixed-Point Reasoners (FPRM, arXiv 2606.18206) switch post-norm to pre-norm and add learnable residual scales α₁, α₂. Theorem 2: if α₂λ_f < 1, f is a contraction with a unique fixed point. α is learnable because "an excessively contractive one severely limits expressivity". Theorem 3 uses the damped update g_η(z;x) = η f(z;x) + (1−η) z, with η reduced geometrically when progress stalls (a patience-based "FPOPT" applied at test time), to remove latent oscillations. Fixed-point convergence serves as the halting signal, giving difficulty-adaptive compute on Sudoku, Maze, state-tracking and ARC-AGI [search excerpt] — [Movahedi et al. 2026](https://arxiv.org/html/2606.18206v1); [ArXivIQ](https://arxiviq.substack.com/p/fixed-point-reasoners-stable-and) [secondary]
- DT-L uses learned linear interpolation for residuals (a form of relaxation) with spectral normalization [search excerpt] — [Bear et al. 2024](https://arxiv.org/html/2410.23451)

**Random iteration counts, truncated backprop, per-pass losses**
- Huginn samples (no-grad steps n, grad steps k) per batch. Schemes include "bptt", "non-uniform", optional linear curricula ("curriculum-N"), and lock-stepped seeds across ranks. The first n steps run under `torch.no_grad()` (truncated backprop). Options exist to divide the loss by the number of grad steps (`mcleish_throttle`) and for per-depth loss weighting (`elbayad_weighing`). Initial-state options: normal, scaled-embed, like-init, zero, unit. Intermediate noise injection with geometric, sqrt or linear decay over steps is also available [primary] — [recpre/model_dynamic.py](https://github.com/seal-rg/recurrent-pretraining/blob/main/recpre/model_dynamic.py)
- Progressive loss (random no-grad prefix, then k trained steps) plus recall is essential for >97% at 512-bit prefix sums [search excerpt] — [Bansal et al. 2022](https://proceedings.neurips.cc/paper_files/paper/2022/file/7f70331dbe58ad59d83941dfa7d975aa-Paper-Conference.pdf)
- Stochastic loop schedules reduce run-to-run OOD variance and stabilize predictions across inference loop counts [search excerpt] — [Kuo et al. 2026](https://arxiv.org/html/2606.29983v1)
- Closing the Loop (Marchenko et al., arXiv 2610.00673, Sep 2026). A 1.4B LoopLM trained on **310B tokens (vs Ouro's 7.7T)** beats a parameter-matched dense model on all 12 benchmarks (+14 GSM8K, +10 MATH, +22 DROP). The dense-to-looped conversion recipe uses a single learned input-mixing scalar and a smoothed exit loss, with no step-specific parameters (Qwen3-1.7B-Base looped beats its dense baseline on all benchmarks). Stability came from pretraining followed by high-quality mid-training, LR warmup and stronger exit-gate regularization [search excerpt] — [Marchenko et al. 2026](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.00673)
- Intermediate-loop supervision in ELT (vision generation) keeps early iterations from being a "black box" [search excerpt] — [ELT](https://arxiv.org/html/2604.09168v2)
- Part II learns the depth prior from prediction feedback with an entropy term and uses orthogonal input injection [search excerpt] — [Huang et al. 2026](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.06833)

**Input re-injection**
- Recall (input concatenated every step) prevents the original problem "being forgotten or corrupted" [search excerpt] — [[Re] study](https://openreview.net/pdf/07d5d68b5873d779bd1fd8c95b9767cb57fe0bc4.pdf)
- Huginn's injection types: add, gate, linear adapter on concat[x, e], FFN adapter, modulated [primary] — [recpre/model_dynamic.py](https://github.com/seal-rg/recurrent-pretraining/blob/main/recpre/model_dynamic.py)
- Input injection improves path independence and accuracy [search excerpt] — [Anil et al. 2022](https://arxiv.org/abs/2211.09961)

### Inferences
- Why damping works (linear analysis consistent with FPRM's condition): with g = ηf + (1−η)I, each Jacobian eigenvalue λ maps to 1 + η(λ−1). For small η this has magnitude below 1 exactly when Re(λ) < 1. Oscillatory modes (complex λ, |λ|≥1, Re λ<1) are therefore removed by a small enough step, at the cost of more passes. Divergent real modes (Re λ>1) cannot be damped. They need a Jacobian or spectral penalty or residual down-scaling (ε=1/N).
- Suggested distillation recipe for a deployed A-B-A-B student, composed from documented levers:
  1. Re-inject the input (or the teacher-aligned embedding) at each cycle.
  2. Sample the loop count per batch (log-normal or uniform around the target, sometimes beyond it), with a no-grad prefix so the student cannot key on a fixed depth.
  3. Randomize the initial state for part of the batch (PI-Net style half random / half zero) so warm starts work.
  4. Add per-pass (or per-cycle) distillation losses with an extra-pass consistency term.
  5. Use sandwich norm or learnable residual scales of order 1/N.
  6. Add a stochastic Jacobian or spectral-radius penalty (DEQ `jac_loss_freq`-style, or STARS).
  7. At deployment, enable damped updates when residuals stall.

  Expected payoffs: early exit (KL- or residual-based), warm starts across tokens and frames, and robustness when running more cycles on hard inputs.
- Trade-off to track: every contraction lever reduces expressivity (FPRM says so explicitly; post-norm "fails to perform deep reasoning"; monDEQ and DT-L accuracy or capacity costs). Track teacher-match quality at a fixed pass count alongside the convergence diagnostics.

### Gaps
- Found no head-to-head measurement of pre-norm vs sandwich vs post-norm on test-time scaling curves in one controlled study, beyond the STARS qualitative statement and Blayney's convergence table.
- Huginn's paper-stated truncation depth (backprop through the last k iterations) and recurrence-sampling mean could not be checked against the PDF here. The code exposes them as `mean_backprop_depth` and `mean_recurrence` config values.
- No published numbers were found for damping's quality or latency cost on looped LLMs (FPRM reports on puzzle tasks).
- Found no stability or test-time-scaling study on looped models running on edge hardware (phones, browsers, Jetson). Numerical precision effects (fp16/int8) on fixed-point convergence are uncharacterized in these sources; RAMP (arXiv 2610.13041, "Recurrent Adaptive Mixed Precision", Oct 2026) appeared in search results but was not examined.
