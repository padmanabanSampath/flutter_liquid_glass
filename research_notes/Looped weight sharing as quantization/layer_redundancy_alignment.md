## Q1. Weight-space similarity between layers (cosine, low-rank structure of differences)

### Takeaway
Raw weights of different layers in a trained LLM are close to orthogonal under naive measures: standard similarity indices barely tell them apart, because weights stay near their (often orthogonal) random initialization. Better-designed indices (DOCS) and head-level comparisons do find real structure. Adjacent layers are more similar than distant ones, layers form clusters, and some individual attention heads across layers are nearly identical. Neuron-level FF similarity *after optimal permutation* is still modest (mean correlation ~0.39 in ViT). This is the strongest evidence that naive averaging fails and that alignment plus a residual (low-rank delta) is needed.

### Cited Findings
- **DOCS (Min & Wang, Alibaba; ICLR 2025, arXiv 2501.16650, Jan 2025).** The authors argue that existing similarity indices "fail to discriminate between orthogonal matrices", which are common in LLMs because of orthogonal initialization. They propose a Distribution-of-Cosine-Similarity index (max |cos| per vector, Gumbel fit) and prove it discriminates in this regime. — [arXiv HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2501.16650v1); [ICLR 2025 proceedings](https://proceedings.iclr.cc/paper_files/paper/2025/hash/a39a9aceda771cded859ae7560530e09-Abstract-Conference.html)
- DOCS heatmaps across LLM layers show higher weight similarity near the diagonal (adjacent layers). Similarity decays as the layer gap grows, and layers form clusters, which the authors read as depth-wise functional specialization. The first and last layers also show elevated similarity to each other, which the authors attribute to their tie to the token embeddings. — [arXiv PDF 2501.16650](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2501.16650)
- **Head-wise Shareable Attention (Cao et al., EMNLP Findings 2024, arXiv 2402.11819).** The paper concatenates each head's W_Q and W_K and compares heads across layers by cosine similarity. It reports that the most similar layer pairs reach ~0.90 while some head pairs reach ~0.99. An ablation found that Q‖K concatenation beats Q-only, K-only or V-based metrics for choosing heads to share **[ablation detail is secondary]**. — [ACL Anthology PDF](https://aclanthology.org/2024.findings-emnlp.144.pdf); [arXiv 2402.11819](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2402.11819); [EmergentMind topic page](https://www.emergentmind.com/topics/similarity-guided-per-head-reuse)
- In the same paper, **DirectShare** reuses pretrained head weights across layers *with no retraining*. It "keeps much of the performance" when the sharing ratio is below 30%. **PostShare** adds a post-training regularizer that pulls paired heads' Q/K together before sharing and does better at larger sharing ratios. — [ACL Anthology PDF](https://aclanthology.org/2024.findings-emnlp.144.pdf); code: [zouyingcao/DirectShare](https://github.com/zouyingcao/DirectShare)
- **Merging FF sublayers (Verma, Murray, Duh; TMLR 2026, arXiv 2501.06126).** Within one model, the average activation correlation between permutation-aligned FF neurons of different layers is 0.385 for ViT FF sublayers and 0.210 for ViT attention, against ~2e-5 for random activations. Alignment finds real but partial correspondence. — [OpenReview TMLR PDF](https://openreview.net/pdf?id=t8iuiH46g0); [arXiv 2501.06126](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2501.06126)
- **Basis Sharing (Wang et al., ICLR 2025, arXiv 2410.03765).** The method represents weight matrices of *different layers* of a pretrained LLM as linear combinations of a shared set of basis vectors with layer-specific coefficients. It also defines a criterion for which layers should share a basis. It beats per-layer SVD compression at 20%–50% compression *without fine-tuning*, with up to 25% lower perplexity on generation than the best SVD baselines. This implies that cross-layer weight *subspaces* overlap even though raw matrices are not close. — [arXiv 2410.03765v1](https://arxiv.org/html/2410.03765v1); [ICLR 2025](https://proceedings.iclr.cc/paper_files/paper/2025/hash/238c98450b1d9e8055f94d22f303bb57-Abstract-Conference.html); code [TUDa-HWAI/Basis_Sharing](https://github.com/TUDa-HWAI/Basis_Sharing)
- **DeltaLLM (Mikaelyan et al., arXiv 2501.18596, Jan 2025).** Later blocks reuse an anchor layer's weights plus a per-layer **low-rank delta** (SVD-initialized, then trained with progressive module replacement while the rest of the model is frozen). Per a summary, a 12% parameter reduction retains ~90% of base average accuracy on common reasoning benchmarks and beats JointDrop, SliceGPT, ShortGPT and LaCo at comparable savings **[secondary]**. MLP weights proved more compressible this way than attention weights. — [arXiv 2501.18596](https://arxiv.org/html/2501.18596v1); [Moonlight review](https://www.themoonlight.io/en/review/deltallm-compress-llms-with-low-rank-deltas-between-shared-weights)
- **LiSA (Cross-layer Attention Sharing for Pre-trained LLMs; TACL 2026, arXiv 2408.01890).** The authors find that "highly similar attention patterns persist within most layers". However, "directly sharing the weight matrix without carefully rearranging the attention heads proves to be ineffective", and "shallow layers are vulnerable to small deviations in attention weights". — [GitHub takagi97/lisa README](https://github.com/takagi97/lisa); [TACL DOI](https://doi.org/10.1162/TACL.a.616); [ACL Anthology 2026.tacl-1.30](https://aclanthology.org/2026.tacl-1.30/)

### Inferences
- Raw-weight cosine between two layers' full matrices is essentially uninformative and near zero. The structure lives in (a) a shared subspace (Basis Sharing), (b) a subset of individual heads (Head-wise sharing), and (c) neuron correspondences that only appear after permutation (Verma et al.). Any looped A→B→A→B conversion should expect each tied pair to need an explicit alignment *and* a per-layer residual such as a low-rank delta. A plain average will not do.
- The DOCS clustering of adjacent layers suggests grouping *contiguous* blocks of similar layers for tying (e.g., tie within clusters). Alternating non-adjacent layers is not suggested by the evidence.
- MLP vs attention compressibility is contested. DeltaLLM finds MLP deltas compress better, while "What Matters in Transformers" (Q2) finds attention layers far more droppable. Both may hold, since dropping differs from sharing.

### Gaps
- I found no paper reporting plain full-matrix cosine similarity numbers (e.g., "adjacent-layer W_up cosine = 0.0x") for Llama-2/3 or Mistral. DOCS has the heatmaps, but I could not extract values because the full text was blocked.
- I found no explicit spectrum or effective-rank measurement of *layer differences* (W_{l+1} − W_l), with or without alignment, for a public LLM. DeltaLLM and Relaxed Recursive Transformers use low-rank deltas but report rank-vs-accuracy only after training.

## Q2. Function-space redundancy: removing or merging layers, with and without healing

### Takeaway
On multiple-choice knowledge benchmarks (MMLU, BoolQ), Llama-2-family models tolerate removing ~25–28% of layers (7B/13B) and up to ~45–55% (70B) with small drops, even *without* retraining. Those numbers overstate retained quality. Generation, next-token loss and commonsense zero-shot averages degrade much more: ShortGPT at 25% on Llama-2-7B goes from 62.8 to 47.4 average zero-shot. Training-free *merging* (LaCo, MKA) beats dropping on MMLU-style tests. Methods that train a small replacement or fusion module (LLM-Streamline, FuseGPT, LiSA, DeltaLLM) recover much more of the lost quality.

### Cited Findings
**Pure removal, no retraining**
- **ShortGPT (Men et al., arXiv 2403.03853, Mar 2024; ACL Findings 2025).** It uses Block Influence (BI = 1 − cosine similarity between a block's input and output hidden states) and removes the lowest-BI layers. Llama-2-13B: removing 10 of 40 layers moves MMLU from 55.0 to 52.22 (~95% retained). — [arXiv 2403.03853v1](https://arxiv.org/html/2403.03853v1)
- ShortGPT ACL version: Llama-2-7B baseline MMLU 45.4 becomes 44.0 at 27.1% layer removal. — [ACL Findings 2025 PDF](https://aclanthology.org/2025.findings-acl.1035.pdf)
- The nine lowest-BI layers of Llama-2-7B are 27, 26, 25, 28, 24, 29, 23, 21, 22: deep but *not* the last two (30, 31) **[secondary: unofficial reimplementation]**. — [GitHub sramshetty/ShortGPT](https://github.com/sramshetty/ShortGPT)
- **Counterpoint on broader tasks (FinerCut, arXiv 2405.18218).** In FinerCut's Llama-2-7B table at 25% layer removal, ShortGPT averages 47.4 on zero-shot tasks against 62.8 for the original (~75% retained). MMLU-only reporting hides much larger losses. — [FinerCut arXiv 2405.18218](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2405.18218)
- **"What Matters in Transformers? Not All Attention is Needed" (He et al., arXiv 2406.15786, 2024).** Training-free "Attention Drop" removes half of Llama-2-70B's attention layers for a **48.4% speedup with a 2.4% performance drop**. Dropping whole blocks or MLP layers degrades performance much more. A source discrepancy exists: the arXiv abstract reportedly cites Llama-3-70B for the half-pruning result. — [OpenReview forum](https://openreview.net/forum?id=YLTWwEjkdx); [OpenReview PDF](https://openreview.net/notes/edits/attachment?id=5mZ6NNFAXl&name=pdf)
- **The Remarkable Robustness of LLMs: Stages of Inference? (Lad, Gurnee, Tegmark; arXiv 2406.19384, v3 Jun 2025).** Deleting a single layer, or swapping two adjacent layers, retains **72–95% of original top-1 prediction accuracy without any fine-tuning**. Deeper models are more robust, and early and final layers are most sensitive. — [arXiv 2406.19384](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2406.19384); [v1 HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2406.19384v1)
- **SLEB (Song et al., ICML 2024, arXiv 2402.09025)** eliminates whole blocks training-free, using a redundancy-verification criterion. A third-party paper (GradMAP, arXiv 2602.14649) reports SLEB on Llama-2-7B at 25% compression averaging 55.96 across seven zero-shot tasks; the dense baseline was not in the extract. — [SLEB arXiv](https://arxiv.org/html/2402.09025); [GradMAP](https://arxiv.org/html/2602.14649v1)

**Removal with healing**
- **The Unreasonable Ineffectiveness of the Deeper Layers (Gromov, Tirumala, Shapourian, Glorioso, Roberts; ICLR 2025, arXiv 2403.17887).** Layers are chosen by the angular distance between the representation at layer ℓ and at ℓ+n, removing the n-block whose input and output are most similar. Healing uses a small QLoRA fine-tune. **Llama-2-70B can lose ~45–55% of layers before MMLU/BoolQ collapse.** Without healing, accuracy stays roughly flat until ~40% of layers are removed and then drops sharply. Healing pushes the collapse point later and restores C4 next-token loss, which otherwise rises steadily. Healed C4 loss keeps rising slowly up to ~80% pruning. QA accuracy is preserved while next-token prediction is clearly disrupted, which the authors flag as a puzzle. — [arXiv HTML v2](https://arxiv.org/html/2403.17887v2); [ICLR 2025 PDF](https://proceedings.iclr.cc/paper_files/paper/2025/file/cbabc2f70de2dd09f491a8715ec3e80f-Paper-Conference.pdf); [IAIFI summary](https://research.iaifi.org/posts/the-unreasonable-ineffectiveness-of-the-deeper-layers/)
- Per-family thresholds in Gromov et al. are roughly Mistral-7B ~35%, Phi-2 ~25% and Qwen ~20% before collapse **[secondary: from a search summary, verify against the paper's figures]**. After healing, the difference between similarity-based pruning and simply dropping the last n layers (excluding the final layer) becomes small. — [arXiv 2403.17887](https://arxiv.org/html/2403.17887v1); [Liner review](https://liner.com/review/the-unreasonable-ineffectiveness-of-the-deeper-layers)
- **Reassessing Layer Pruning in LLMs (Lu et al., arXiv 2411.15558; ICLR 2026).** Recipe: prune the *final 25%* of layers of Llama-3.1-8B-It, then fine-tune only the lm_head plus the last three remaining layers. This beats prior SOTA pruning methods by 5.62–17.27% on Llama-3.1-8B-It. Partial-layer fine-tuning outperforms LoRA healing. — [arXiv 2411.15558](https://arxiv.org/html/2411.15558); [ICLR 2026 PDF](https://proceedings.iclr.cc/paper_files/paper/2026/file/40624bef9a7d22c0c780dfe9291f5843-Paper-Conference.pdf); [GitHub](https://github.com/yaolu-zjut/Navigation_LLM_layer_pruning)

**Merging layers instead of dropping**
- **LaCo: Layer Collapse (Yang et al., EMNLP Findings 2024, arXiv 2402.11187).** Rear layers are folded into a prior layer by adding their parameter differences to it; no training is required. Authors report keeping average task performance >80% at 25–30% pruning while baselines stay <70%. One summary instead says ">91% of Llama-2-7B performance at 25%", so these figures conflict **[secondary]**. — [ACL Anthology PDF](https://aclanthology.org/2024.findings-emnlp.372.pdf); [Moonlight review](https://www.themoonlight.io/en/review/laco-large-language-model-pruning-via-layer-collapse)
- **MKA: Pruning via Merging / Manifold-based Knowledge Alignment (Liu et al., EMNLP 2024, arXiv 2406.16330).** Each layer's activations are embedded with a diffusion kernel (manifold learning), layer similarity is computed with a Normalized Pairwise Information Bottleneck (NPIB), and the most similar layers are fused by **weighted blending of their weights**, with no retraining. **Llama-3-8B on MMLU: 43.75% compression (14 of 32 layers) with a 2.82% performance drop.** At 34.375% compression, MKA beats ShortGPT by 21.92% and SparseGPT by 20.42% on Llama-3-8B MMLU. One preprint version says 43.5%. Whether the 2.82% is absolute or relative was not confirmed. — [ACL Anthology](https://aclanthology.org/2024.emnlp-main.987/); [OpenReview](https://openreview.net/forum?id=LHfAyDyHcE); [GitHub README (pipeline: activations → diffusion kernel → NPIB similarity → weight blending by fusion ratio)](https://github.com/SempraETY/Pruning-via-Merging)
- **LLM-Streamline (Chen et al., ICLR 2025, arXiv 2403.19135).** A contiguous run of redundant layers is replaced by one *trained* lightweight network. At a 25% pruning rate on 7B/13B models it keeps ~93% of performance on classification and ~77% on generation, and beats LaCo by ~5 points on Llama-2-7B. — [ICLR 2025 PDF](https://proceedings.iclr.cc/paper_files/paper/2025/file/4b00a351b41358965613c118e87dc28c-Paper-Conference.pdf); [arXiv 2403.19135](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2403.19135)
- **FuseGPT / "From Pruning to Grafting" (arXiv 2411.14507, Nov 2024).** Learnable low-rank fusion grafts the knowledge of removed blocks into neighboring blocks, guided by a fusion-aware importance metric, so it requires training on calibration data. At 25% sparsity it gets lower perplexity than prior methods at 20%, up to +4.5 zero-shot points, 1.33× speedup and 25% memory reduction. Combined with 4-bit GPTQ it reaches 52.1% total compression. Tested on Llama, Mistral, Qwen and Phi. — [arXiv 2411.14507](https://arxiv.org/html/2411.14507)
- **LiSA (TACL 2026).** Shares attention across adjacent layers using a tiny head-alignment FFN plus low-rank difference matrices. It replaces attention computation in **53–84% of layers**, compresses Q and K **6×**, and raises throughput by +19.5% (Llama-3-8B), +32.3% (Llama-2-7B) and +40.1% (Llama-2-13B). Quality stays comparable across 13 benchmarks, including math. It trains only 0.46–1.64% of parameters (56M for Llama-3-8B, on one 80 GB GPU) with ~4.2B tokens of RedPajama-derived uptraining data. — [GitHub README](https://github.com/takagi97/lisa); [TACL DOI](https://doi.org/10.1162/TACL.a.616)

**Painters: weight-repetition evidence directly relevant to looping**
- **Transformer Layers as Painters (Sun et al., arXiv 2407.09298; AAAI 2025).** In Llama-2-7B, middle layers are robust to skipping and even reordering. For example, feeding layer 4's output into layer 7 works, while skipping early layers is catastrophic. Crucially, **replacing a set of middle layers with copies of a single middle layer's weights causes significant drops, worse than simply skipping those layers.** Running middle layers in parallel and looping the parallel block partially recovers. Reordering hurts math and reasoning tasks most. — [arXiv 2407.09298v3](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2407.09298v3); [AAAI version](https://ojs.aaai.org/index.php/AAAI/article/view/34708/36863); [Emergence AI blog](https://www.emergence.ai/blog/exploring-the-functional-roles-of-transformer-layers)

### Inferences
- For a looped A→B→A→B conversion, the Painters result is the most direct warning. Naively reusing one middle layer's weights at several depths is *worse than deleting those depths*. Tying needs merged or aligned weights plus per-depth corrections, not repetition.
- MMLU-only evaluations (ShortGPT, MKA) are optimistic. Gromov et al. show QA is preserved while next-token loss degrades, and FinerCut's 62.8 → 47.4 shows zero-shot averages fall sharply at 25% removal. A sharing study should measure perplexity and generation, not just MMLU.
- Approximate budget from the evidence: ~25% of layers (Llama-2-7B/13B, Llama-3-8B) can be removed or merged training-free with a small MMLU loss but a large generative loss. With light healing (QLoRA or partial-layer fine-tuning), the usable fraction for 70B-class models reaches ~40–50%. Training-free merging (MKA, LaCo) beats dropping at the same ratio, which hints that weight-space blending retains *some* useful signal between similar layers.

### Gaps
- Exact Gromov healing budget (QLoRA rank, C4 steps or tokens) and the per-model thresholds for Llama-2-7B/13B and Llama-3-8B could not be extracted because arXiv was blocked. The paper reportedly runs on a single A100, but this was not re-verified.
- I found no consistent head-to-head table for Mistral-7B covering ShortGPT, SLEB, LaCo, MKA and LLM-Streamline at the same ratio.
- The FuseGPT, LLM-Streamline and Reassessing healing-token budgets were not extracted.

## Q3. Which layers are most redundant, and the residual-stream view

### Takeaway
There is strong consensus. The first one to few layers and the last one to two layers are critical. Middle-to-late layers, roughly the second half excluding the final layer(s), are the most redundant: they make small residual updates and live in a shared representation space. Pre-LayerNorm training is a plausible cause, because it drives deep blocks toward identity-like Jacobians.

### Cited Findings
- **Painters:** cosine similarity of hidden states across Llama-2-13B's 40 layers forms 4–5 groups: layer 0, layers 1–3, the middle layers, and the final 1–2 layers. Middle layers share a common representation space, plausibly due to residual connections. — [arXiv 2407.09298](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2407.09298v3)
- **Lad et al.:** interventions on early and final layers cause the most degradation, and models are "remarkably robust" to dropping middle layers. They propose four stages of inference: detokenization, feature engineering, prediction ensembling, and residual sharpening. — [arXiv 2406.19384](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2406.19384)
- **ShortGPT:** the lowest-BI layers of Llama-2-7B are 21–29, deep but not the last two **[secondary]**. — [GitHub sramshetty/ShortGPT](https://github.com/sramshetty/ShortGPT)
- **Gromov et al.:** the optimal blocks to remove are deep, but the final layer must be kept. — [arXiv 2403.17887v2](https://arxiv.org/html/2403.17887v2)
- **Csordás, Manning, Potts, "Do Language Models Use Their Depth Efficiently?" (arXiv 2505.13898, May 2025).** On Llama 3.1 and Qwen 3, second-half layers contribute much less than first-half layers, with a clear phase transition between the halves. Skipping second-half layers barely affects later computation and outputs. — [arXiv 2505.13898](https://arxiv.org/html/2505.13898v1)
- **The Curse of Depth in LLMs (Sun et al., arXiv 2502.05795, Feb 2025).** Pre-LN output variance grows exponentially with depth, so deep blocks' derivatives approach the identity. LayerNorm Scaling (scaling the LN output by 1/√depth) fixes this and improves pre-training from 130M to 7B parameters. — [arXiv 2502.05795](https://arxiv.org/html/2502.05795v1)
- **"Do Depth-Grown Models Overcome the Curse of Depth?" (arXiv 2512.08819, Dec 2025).** Models grown by gradual middle stacking use their depth more effectively. — [arXiv 2512.08819](https://arxiv.org/html/2512.08819v1)
- **LiSA:** shallow layers are most sensitive to small deviations in attention weights. — [GitHub README](https://github.com/takagi97/lisa)
- **Reassessing Layer Pruning:** simply pruning the final 25% of layers (keeping lm_head) is a strong baseline once the last remaining layers are fine-tuned. — [arXiv 2411.15558](https://arxiv.org/html/2411.15558)

### Inferences
- The best candidates for sharing are contiguous blocks in the middle and late-middle range, roughly layers 25–90% of depth. Keep the first ~1–3 and the last ~1–2 layers untied, since every source agrees they are distinct and sensitive.
- Because middle layers make small residual updates (identity-dominated Jacobians under Pre-LN), *functional* differences between tied middle layers may be small even when weights differ. This favours sharing via function-matching objectives (distillation or activation matching) over weight-matching.
- Middle-stacked or grown models, and models trained with LayerNorm Scaling, may be *less* redundant and therefore harder to compress by sharing. This hypothesis has not been tested.

### Gaps
- I found no per-layer redundancy curves for Mistral-7B specifically, beyond Gromov's family-level thresholds.

## Q4. Symmetries and alignment (permutation, rotation) and whether anyone aligns *across layers of the same model*

### Takeaway
Yes, but only recently and only for sublayers. **Verma, Murray & Duh (TMLR 2026)** permutation-align adjacent FF sublayers within one model, then average and tie them. **LiSA (TACL 2026)** and **Head-wise Shareable Attention (EMNLP-F 2024)** align or match attention heads across layers before sharing. I found no paper that applies continuous **rotation or Procrustes** alignment across layers of one model before tying. Rotation-based matching exists for *cross-model* fusion (ICML 2025) and for pruning compensation (2026), so that is an open gap.

### Cited Findings
**Within-model, cross-layer alignment (directly on point)**
- **Merging Feed-Forward Sublayers for Compressed Transformers (Verma, Murray, Duh; arXiv 2501.06126 Jan 2025; TMLR 2026).**
  - Pipeline: (1) compute activation cross-correlations between FF sublayers (activations taken just before the nonlinearity, up to 10k tokens); (2) choose an anchor sublayer (first, middle or last; first is the default); (3) solve a linear assignment problem (Jonker–Volgenant) per sublayer to permute its hidden units to match the anchor; (4) average the permuted weights; (5) tie them to one stored copy; (6) run short recovery fine-tuning. Sublayer selection is a sliding window over adjacent sublayers, keeping the best window on validation data.
  - Models: ViT, GPT-2 (large, 36 layers), OLMo-1B, OPUS-MT, and OLMo3-7B with QLoRA (r=8, α=16, all-linear, 3,000 updates; tasks SamSum, NarrativeQA, HotPotQA). ViT recovery fine-tuning uses up to 50k updates per the repo script.
  - Results: tying more than a third of FF sublayers gives comparable performance and beats a layer-dropping baseline. ViT: merging 4 of 12 FF sublayers removes >21% of parameters and keeps ~99% of ImageNet accuracy (after fine-tuning). GPT-2: merging 12 of 36 FF sublayers raises test perplexity by roughly one point **[secondary review]**. It is compatible with LLM.int8() quantization.
  - The repo includes CKA similarity analysis for attention and FF sublayers.
  - Sources: [GitHub nverma1/merging-ffs-compression README](https://github.com/nverma1/merging-ffs-compression); [OpenReview TMLR](https://openreview.net/pdf?id=t8iuiH46g0); [arXiv 2501.06126](https://arxiv.org/html/2501.06126v2); [Pith review](https://pith.science/paper/2501.06126)
- **LiSA:** a learned tiny FFN (two-layer, [2·num_heads, 256] → [256, num_heads]) aligns attention heads between adjacent layers, choosing the most similar heads in the shared matrix, and low-rank matrices compensate for remaining differences. Direct sharing without head rearrangement "is akin to random permutation" and fails. Training loss is 0.75·CE + 0.25·MSE. — [GitHub README](https://github.com/takagi97/lisa); [arXiv 2408.01890](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2408.01890)
- **Head-wise Shareable Attention:** matches heads across layers by Q‖K weight cosine and shares the matched pairs. This is a discrete matching across layers that works without retraining below ~30% sharing. — [ACL Anthology PDF](https://aclanthology.org/2024.findings-emnlp.144.pdf)
- **MiniViT (CVPR 2022):** shares weights across consecutive ViT blocks plus small per-layer transformations, trained with weight distillation (details in Q6). — [CVF PDF](https://openaccess.thecvf.com/content/CVPR2022/papers/Zhang_MiniViT_Compressing_Vision_Transformers_With_Weight_Multiplexing_CVPR_2022_paper.pdf)

**Cross-model alignment methods (transferable tools)**
- **Merging Text Transformer Models from Different Initializations (Verma & Elbayad, arXiv 2403.00986, 2024).** Permutation-aligns FF sublayers, multi-head attention (head and within-head) and the residual stream between independently trained MultiBERTs. Compared with vanilla averaging (no permutation), aligning either FF or attention reduces pseudo-perplexity, and aligning both cuts it by **almost 7× at λ=0.5**. — [arXiv 2403.00986](https://arxiv.org/html/2403.00986)
- **Beyond the Permutation Symmetry of Transformers: The Role of Rotation for Model Fusion (Zhang, Zheng, Chen, Li; ICML 2025, arXiv 2502.00264).** Self-attention has a *continuous rotation symmetry*: rotating the Q/K (and V/O) parameter blocks leaves the function unchanged, which generalizes head permutation. Matching uses a closed-form Kabsch / orthogonal-Procrustes solution for attention, with FF still matched by permutation. Plug-and-play, it substantially improves fusion of pretrained NLP and vision transformers. — [arXiv 2502.00264](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2502.00264); [PMLR v267](https://proceedings.mlr.press/v267/zhang25dk.html)
- **SLOA: Training-Free Transformer Merging via Sequential Local Operator Alignment (arXiv 2610.06415, Oct 2026).** Merging layers independently breaks attention, because the query–key and value–output operators are *composed* matrices. Merging earlier components also shifts downstream activations, so SLOA merges sequentially along the execution path with calibration data. — [arXiv 2610.06415](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.06415)
- **OrthoMerge / Orthogonal Model Merging (arXiv 2602.05943, 2026).** Solves a one-sided orthogonal Procrustes problem per expert and per weight matrix (rotation on the input space), averages the rotations on the orthogonal group, and merges residuals additively. — [alphaXiv 2602.05943](https://www.alphaxiv.org/abs/2602.05943)
- **CHASE (arXiv 2610.09476, Oct 2026).** After structural pruning, uses an orthogonal Procrustes rotation to align the retained weights' outputs with the original layer's outputs on calibration activations. — [arXiv 2610.09476](https://arxiv.org/html/2610.09476v1)
- A 2026 interpretability paper argues that independently trained transformers compute the same function in residual-stream bases differing by a uniform random rotation on SO(d_model), removable by one Procrustes fit **[secondary]**. — [Pith 2605.24577](https://pith.science/paper/2605.24577)
- A non-local merging paper (arXiv 2410.12766) notes that exact weight-matching permutation minimization is NP-hard for deep networks; practical methods solve layer-wise LAPs. — [arXiv 2410.12766](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2410.12766)
- **[prior knowledge]** *Git Re-Basin* (Ainsworth et al., ICLR 2023, arXiv 2209.04836) introduced weight matching (LAP on weight similarity), activation matching (LAP on activation correlation) and a straight-through estimator. *ZipIt!* (Stoica et al., ICLR 2024, arXiv 2305.03053) generalizes this by allowing features to be merged *within* each model as well as across models, plus partial zipping up to a chosen layer. *SliceGPT* (Ashkboos et al., ICLR 2024, arXiv 2401.15024) uses "computational invariance": after folding LayerNorm into RMSNorm, any orthogonal Q applied to the residual stream (W_in ← QᵀW_in, W_out ← W_out Q) leaves the network unchanged. SliceGPT uses PCA rotations to slice up to ~25% of parameters. *QuaRot* (arXiv 2404.00456) and *SpinQuant* exploit the same invariance with Hadamard or learned rotations for quantization. — [Git Re-Basin](https://arxiv.org/abs/2209.04836); [ZipIt!](https://arxiv.org/abs/2305.03053); [SliceGPT](https://arxiv.org/abs/2401.15024); [QuaRot](https://arxiv.org/abs/2404.00456)

### Inferences
- **Which symmetries can actually align layer i to layer j inside one model?** The residual-stream rotation used by SliceGPT/QuaRot is *global*: all layers read and write one stream, so one Q is applied to every layer and cannot make two layers more alike. The *per-layer internal* symmetries are free:
  - permutation of MLP hidden units, including the paired gate/up rows and down columns for SwiGLU;
  - permutation of attention heads, within a GQA group constraint;
  - per-head invertible maps on the QK pair (W_Q A, W_K A^{-T}); with RoPE, only maps commuting with the rotary blocks are allowed, i.e., per-frequency 2×2 rotations or permutations;
  - per-head invertible maps on the VO pair (W_V B, B^{-1} W_O);
  - RMSNorm gain vectors, which can be folded into the following linear layer.

  These are exactly what Verma et al. (FF permutation), LiSA/Head-wise (head matching) and the ICML 2025 rotation paper (QK/VO rotation) exploit. A looped-conversion pipeline can align every to-be-tied layer to an anchor using these per-layer symmetries before averaging, at zero function cost.
- **Activation matching vs weight matching.** Verma et al. and Git Re-Basin-style activation matching are natural for within-model alignment, because the layers being tied see *different* inputs. Weight matching ignores input-distribution shift, while correlation on each layer's own inputs accounts for it. SLOA's warning about composed QK/VO operators argues for aligning those as pairs (as in ICML 2025) rather than per matrix.
- Even after optimal permutation, ViT FF neurons correlate only ~0.39 on average across layers. Alignment should be seen as a better initialization for averaging plus delta, not as making layers equal. Budget a per-layer low-rank residual (Relaxed RT / DeltaLLM / LiSA style) and some healing.
- **Novelty gap:** no published work (found) combines (a) continuous QK/VO rotation alignment across layers of one model, (b) FF permutation alignment, and (c) a tied looped schedule such as A→B→A→B. This appears to be open territory as of 2026-10.

### Gaps
- The Verma et al. ablation of permutation vs no-permutation (naive averaging) for *within-model* FF merging, and its numbers *before* recovery fine-tuning, could not be extracted. The paper likely contains them, since a "notune" script exists in the repo.
- I found no study of whether RoPE restricts QK rotation alignment across layers in Llama-style models; the RoPE constraint above is my own inference.
- I found no work aligning entire blocks (attention plus FF jointly, sharing one residual basis) across layers before weight sharing.

## Q5. Looped-model conversion: how badly naive (averaged) initialization performs before uptraining

### Takeaway
Conversion from a pretrained model clearly beats random initialization, but every successful conversion paper uses billions of uptraining tokens. Relaxed Recursive Transformers found *Average* initialization best only when combined with per-layer SVD-initialized LoRA deltas. For a strict tie with no LoRA, *Stepwise* (picking a subset of original layers) beat averaging. Exact zero-step (pre-uptraining) numbers were not retrievable.

### Cited Findings
- **Relaxed Recursive Transformers (Bae et al., Google DeepMind/KAIST; arXiv 2410.20672, Oct 2024; ICLR 2025).**
  - Initializations tested: Stepwise (take every k-th layer), Average (average the weights of layers that will be tied), and Lower (take the first layers).
  - **Stepwise was best for plain recursive (strictly tied) models. Average was best for Relaxed models**, whose per-depth LoRA modules are SVD-initialized on the residual between each original layer and the tied weights. SVD-LoRA init beat zero init by up to 6.5 accuracy points **[secondary]**.
  - Brief uptraining used 15B tokens. Recursive Gemma 1B (converted from Gemma 2B) gained up to +13.5 points of few-shot accuracy (22% error reduction) over a same-size Gemma 1B trained from scratch.
  - With 60B tokens plus knowledge distillation, the relaxed rank-512 Gemma reached 58.4% against 58.6% for the original Gemma 2B, which was pretrained on 3T tokens.
  - Uptraining on SlimPajama itself lowered Gemma's few-shot scores, a data-mismatch effect.
  - Sources: [arXiv 2410.20672 HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2410.20672v1); [arXiv PDF](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2410.20672); [Moonlight review](https://www.themoonlight.io/en/review/relaxed-recursive-transformers-effective-parameter-sharing-with-layer-wise-lora)
- **Teaching Pretrained LMs to Think Deeper with Retrofitted Recurrence (McLeish et al., arXiv 2511.07384, Nov 2025).** Converts Llama-3.2-1B into a (prelude 2, recurrent 4, coda 2) depth-recurrent model, using a curriculum that increases recurrences during training. Initializing from pretrained Llama weights gives significantly higher accuracy than random init at every recurrence level. Loss curves are extrapolated to need ≥~950B tokens before random init catches up. — [arXiv 2511.07384](https://arxiv.org/abs/2511.07384)
- **2026 follow-ups:** "Retrofitting Recurrent Depth into a Pretrained LM" (arXiv 2608.11233) converts Qwen2.5-0.5B, including a 6M-parameter adapter variant over frozen base weights. LoopUS (arXiv 2605.11011) recasts pretrained LLMs into looped latent-refinement models via post-training. It notes that prior retrofits "involve substantial computational costs and may compromise pretrained capabilities". — [arXiv 2608.11233](https://arxiv.org/html/2608.11233); [LoopUS arXiv 2605.11011](https://arxiv.org/html/2605.11011v1)
- **Painters:** repeating a single middle layer's weights in place of several middle layers is worse than skipping them. This is the training-free extreme of naive sharing. — [arXiv 2407.09298](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2407.09298v3)
- **MiniViT:** plain weight-shared ViTs drop significantly in accuracy relative to the originals; per-layer transformations plus distillation are needed to recover. — [CVF PDF](https://openaccess.thecvf.com/content/CVPR2022/papers/Zhang_MiniViT_Compressing_Vision_Transformers_With_Weight_Multiplexing_CVPR_2022_paper.pdf)

### Inferences
- Stepwise beating Average for strict ties is consistent with Q1: unaligned layers are near-orthogonal, so averaging them shrinks and blurs weights, roughly a 1/√k norm reduction for k near-orthogonal matrices. Picking one real layer keeps a coherent function. Averaging only wins when LoRA deltas restore each layer's specifics. Permutation/rotation alignment before averaging (Q4) should make Average (aligned) dominate Stepwise even for strict ties. This is an untested hypothesis and a natural experiment.
- The realistic healing budget for usable looped conversions is billions of tokens: 4.2B (LiSA), 15B to 60B with KD (Relaxed RT), or more (McLeish). That is orders of magnitude more than the QLoRA healing used for layer *dropping*.

### Gaps
- Exact zero-step (pre-uptraining) accuracy or perplexity for Stepwise vs Average vs Lower in Relaxed Recursive Transformers could not be extracted because arXiv was blocked. The paper's initialization figure likely has it.
- I found no paper that measures aligned-average initialization for a strict looped model.

## Q6. Vision transformers (DeiT/ViT/DINOv2) and video models

### Takeaway
ViTs have *more uniform* layer-to-layer representations than CNNs (high CKA between lower and higher layers), which favours sharing. MiniViT showed weight sharing across consecutive blocks works but only with per-layer transforms and distillation. The only within-model *permutation-aligned* layer merge with ViT numbers is Verma et al.: 4 of 12 FF sublayers merged, >21% of parameters removed, ~99% of accuracy kept after fine-tuning. Video evidence is thin. A 2026 paper reports extreme block redundancy in VideoMAE-B.

### Cited Findings
- **Do Vision Transformers See Like CNNs? (Raghu et al., NeurIPS 2021, arXiv 2108.08810).** ViTs have a much more uniform CKA structure across depth than ResNets, with high similarity between lower and higher layers. The authors attribute this to self-attention's early global aggregation and strong residual connections that propagate features upward. CKA is invariant to orthogonal transformations, including neuron permutation. — [NeurIPS PDF](https://proceedings.neurips.cc/paper/2021/file/652cf38361a209088302ba2b8b7f51e0-Paper.pdf); [arXiv 2108.08810](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2108.08810)
- **MiniViT (Zhang et al., CVPR 2022, arXiv 2204.07154).** Weight multiplexing shares weights across consecutive blocks, adds per-layer weight transformations to restore diversity, and trains with weight distillation. Swin-B becomes 48% smaller while gaining +1.0% top-1 on ImageNet. DeiT-B is compressed 9.7×, from 86M to 9M parameters, with 79.8% top-1 reported **[secondary for the 79.8 figure]**. Plain weight sharing without transformations drops accuracy significantly. — [CVF PDF](https://openaccess.thecvf.com/content/CVPR2022/papers/Zhang_MiniViT_Compressing_Vision_Transformers_With_Weight_Multiplexing_CVPR_2022_paper.pdf); [arXiv 2204.07154](https://arxiv.org/abs/2204.07154)
- **Verma et al. (TMLR 2026), ViT:** merging 4 of 12 FF sublayers removes >21% of total parameters and keeps 99% of ImageNet accuracy after recovery fine-tuning. Mean aligned activation correlation is 0.385 for FF and 0.210 for attention. — [GitHub README](https://github.com/nverma1/merging-ffs-compression); [OpenReview](https://openreview.net/pdf?id=t8iuiH46g0)
- **Rotation-symmetry fusion (ICML 2025)** was validated on vision as well as language transformers. — [PMLR](https://proceedings.mlr.press/v267/zhang25dk.html)
- **DINOv2 depth pruning (HetDPT, "Rethinking Depth Pruning for Vision Transformers: A Heterogeneity-Aware Perspective").** DINOv2-Giant: pruning 15 layers gives a 1.26× speedup with a 0.06% accuracy dip, with fine-tuning and optional self-distillation. Its merge step folds two FFN linears into one after removing the GELU between them **[secondary]**. — [Lacuna summary](https://lacuna.tiptreesystems.com/work/rethinking-depth-pruning-for-vision-transformers-a-heterogeneity-aware/wrk_13c7b69532b4e2543066d1ef7e88db43)
- **Elastic ViTs from Pretrained Models without Retraining (arXiv 2510.17700, Oct 2025).** Retraining- and label-free (width/FFN/attention) pruning of DINO, SigLIPv2, DeiT and AugReg. Blocks 8–12 are the most prunable. — [arXiv 2510.17700](https://arxiv.org/html/2510.17700v1)
- A Springer 2025 chapter proposes **training-free fusion of consecutive ViT layers based on activation similarity**; details (models, metric, numbers) could not be retrieved. — [Springer](https://link.springer.com/chapter/10.1007/978-3-032-03072-6_17)
- **Video: "Entropy Reveals Block Importance in Masked Self-Supervised ViTs" (Gardener, arXiv 2602.03918, Feb 2026).** Data-free, entropy-based block pruning of **VideoMAE-B** keeps competitive *transfer* performance even with up to **91.7% of blocks removed**. That is presumably after downstream fine-tuning; the protocol was not verified. — [arXiv 2602.03918](https://arxiv.org/html/2602.03918v1)
- **Video: SVT, Supertoken Video Transformer.** Merges semantically similar latent representations along network depth in video ViTs (ViT-B/L, Kinetics-400), giving gains with fewer FLOPs. This is token-level, not weight sharing. — [PapersWithCode](https://www.paperswithcode.com/paper/svt-supertoken-video-transformer-for)

### Inferences
- ViTs and DINOv2 look at least as amenable to cross-layer sharing as LLMs, given uniform CKA and the MiniViT/Verma results. Late-middle blocks (8–12 of 12 in ViT-B-scale models) are the natural candidates.
- The VideoMAE-B 91.7% figure suggests masked-autoencoder video encoders are heavily over-deep for transfer. It measures transfer after fine-tuning, so it says little about training-free sharing.

### Gaps
- I found no paper giving training-free layer-drop or merge accuracy curves for DeiT or DINOv2 comparable to ShortGPT/Gromov for LLMs.
- I found no cross-layer permutation or rotation alignment results for video transformers (ViViT, VideoMAE, TimeSformer) or for DiT/video diffusion backbones. The search surfaced a 2025 diffusion-transformer layer-pruning-with-distillation paper (arXiv 2511.16156) but nothing on alignment.
